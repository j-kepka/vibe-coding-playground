# AI Transformation Outcomes Playbook

Document outcomes rather than opinions about AI. Use the [template](AI_TRANSFORMATION_OUTCOMES_TEMPLATE.md) to describe one completed intervention in a concise case study. Individual learning projects are valid cases; identify them as such. Each case records how its author worked with AI, what resulted and what was learned.

## What to record

- State the problem, previous process, participants and known baseline.
- Record requirements, constraints and success criteria briefly. They may evolve through iterations; label retrospective accounts instead of presenting later decisions as an upfront specification.
- Identify the case author for attribution. The confidentiality check concerns clients and other participants, confidential information and private personal data about other people.
- Describe what people requested, decided or evaluated and what the AI tool implemented or checked. Attribute their work explicitly.
- Include one or two actual decisions, AI corrections or resolved problems within **Intervention**; separate sections are unnecessary for a small case.
- Separate delivered features, verification results and user feedback from business impact.
- **Business impact** is optional for a commercial or organizational context. Use **Not applicable** for a hobby project and **Not measured** when relevant data is unavailable.
- Optional professional or portfolio value can apply to hobby projects too. Describe capabilities supported by evidence separately from business impact; do not infer revenue or ROI from them.
- Label measurements, owner estimates and historical observations. Include their source, scope and limitations. Use **Not measured** when data is unavailable.
- An effort estimate is not time saved. Claim savings only against a comparable baseline; an estimated manual build time is a counterfactual, not measured savings.
- Link to available, publicly shareable evidence: code, reproducible checks, screenshots or approved feedback. Attribute evidence to the implementation and stage actually checked.
- Explain where human judgment, testing and accountability mattered. A brief reflection on what could be done differently fits **Human lesson**. Identify one reusable asset and one next experiment.

Keep documentation proportional to completed work. Do not invent dates, feedback or productivity measurements. If historical test artifacts were not retained, state that limitation.

Changes to the template do not require rewriting closed historical cases. Preserve reports and milestone records as accounts of their time.

## Status and review

**Draft** means the account or its evidence still needs review. **Validated** means the description and supporting evidence have been checked against the relevant implementation, with limitations stated. **Published** means the reviewed case has actually been made public.

Check confidentiality, attribution, metric accuracy and any required client or organizational permission to publish or reuse the case. For a personal project that requires no such approval, mark permission as **N/A**. This check is separate from choosing a project license and deciding when to publish.

An unchecked item remains visible; completing the checklist does not replace evidence or any required approval.

## A 20-minute routine

1. **0–3 minutes:** choose one case and state the problem.
2. **3–7 minutes:** record the baseline, requirements and intervention.
3. **7–12 minutes:** add results and evidence; label unknowns.
4. **12–16 minutes:** capture the human lesson, reusable asset and next opportunity.
5. **16–20 minutes:** review accuracy, confidentiality, links and status; save the draft.

Commit or publish only when requested and ready for review.
